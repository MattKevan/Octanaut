## Sample editor detail

The reference places the waveform above five rows: buffer size and range bounds; display/range playback and zoom; buffer, monitor and recording tools; range, loop and filter controls; and cut/copy/paste, noise, echo and export. The browser version keeps its input selector, stop and additional editing tools under More controls.

Change-volume accepts start and end percentages for fades; CUT now removes the selected audio and PASTE inserts the buffer at the cursor. Cut and paste were checked on the default fixture sample, and loop positions are adjusted around edits. Range selection accepts right-drag and shift-adjustment. Pitch and destination periods can also be set using tracker note keys, and CHANGE RATE is available; conversion from 428 to 214 was checked with the fixture sample. Separate loop start/end selection, marker dragging, stepping and zero-crossing searches are present; the end marker step was checked in the browser. Noise level, echo rate in bytes, echo decay and echo count are editable, and D.PIXELS switches the waveform to the faster pixel display without touching the sound. Freehand drawing was checked on the fixture sample. Empty sample slots can allocate a silent buffer; resizing preserves stereo channel lengths and clamps loop bounds. A view-position strip and left/right scrolling are present for zoomed samples; the scrolling increment and visual treatment still need comparison with OctaMED. Note-key pitch entry is covered by an interaction test but has not had a browser or original-app comparison. The two number gadgets beside FILT/BOOST are the averaging strength and distance; the V5 manual documents both (defaults 16 and 1), and they now drive the filter and boost weights. Exact effect algorithms and original-device recording behaviour remain gaps. The unpacked executable contains unrelocated binary segments, so those details have not been verified against original source.

## Synth editor detail

The reference shows the current waveform and buffer side by side, with copy, exchange, mix, add, edit-side selection and undo between them. Waveform selection, transform, shapes, speed controls and the volume/waveform programs sit underneath. The editor now follows that grouping and was checked with the SyntHarp1 synthsound from `doctor strange - high score.mmd0`.

Waveform length, add/delete here and last, right-drag range selection, cursor positioning, editable range boundaries with increment/decrement buttons, range copy/paste/clear/double/reverse/shift, volume change, and pixel/line drawing with mix mode are available. Reverse and undo, buffer drawing, and range copy and paste were checked with a fixture synthsound in the browser. CUT moves the selected range to the right-hand buffer and leaves a gap, STRETCH moves the point under the cursor by a signed number of samples, and CLR/ALL empty the current waveform or the whole synthsound; CUT, STRETCH and CLR were exercised on the Synthstring2 fixture. Stretch follows the manual's description rather than a comparison with the original, so its exact easing still needs checking. Boundary buttons need visual and original-app interaction verification. PASTE currently overwrites to the end of the waveform; the original's exact edge and length behaviour still needs verification, as does undo across all operations. OctaMED was launched from an isolated disk copy, but emulator pointer scaling prevented a reliable interaction comparison, so those questions remain open. Sequence lists accept the documented command keys and hexadecimal values, protect END, and adjust same-list and cross-list jump targets on insertion and deletion; insertion and command entry were checked in the browser, and a fixture edit was saved and reloaded as MMD1. Exact original cursor movement and insertion behaviour still need comparison. The waveform displays now include a dotted grid, zero-line ticks and side scales, and the range-boundary controls sit beside the sequence editors. The waveform is drawn as individual sample points, as in the reference. Exact proportions and point appearance still need visual comparison.

## Status strip

The E1/E2/E3 graphic V.U. meters were resolved from OctaMED running in Copperline and from the measured geometry in the OctaMEDIC source: E1 is a bar graph under the info line with slots for instruments 01-31 and for notes from C-1, raised by note triggers and falling like a V.U.; E2 is the V.U. meter at each track's edge from mid-height down, red at the top through yellow to green with grey edges; E3 is the per-track oscilloscope, 42 samples wide, hanging off the highlighted line. All three turn off in split channel modes, and a right click away from the block editor switches them off together. NRM on the memory strip cycles normal, 2/3 and 1/2 playback speed. The info line also carries editable block and song names, as the original does.

The status bar follows the manual: the sequence index and block number step by the place value of the digit clicked (1, 10, 100, 1000), the block click brings that block into the editor while the sequence click only moves the list position, the octave pair loops on left click, and E, SP and CHRD toggle edit mode, note spacing and chord entry. Esc toggles edit mode and `~` toggles SP from the keyboard. The instrument number advances on left click and goes back on right click, the song number steps through songs in memory, and the track lamps toggle mute.
